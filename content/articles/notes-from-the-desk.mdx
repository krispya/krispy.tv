![An aquarium — a contained world where things drift](../images/aquarium.webp)

Most blogs present a reverse-chronological feed. It is the obvious shape: newest on top, scroll for more. But feeds imply throughput — they reward frequency and punish gaps. I wanted something that felt more like a surface than a stream.

The desk metaphor came from a simple observation: when I am thinking through a problem, I spread pages across a table so I can see several ideas at once. Adjacency does work that sequence cannot. Two unrelated notes placed side by side sometimes reveal a connection that a linear list would hide.

So the homepage scatters articles like loose pages. They drift, overlap, and respond to the pointer. The layout is not optimized for scanning — it is optimized for lingering. If a title catches your eye you can pull it closer; if nothing does, the breeze will shuffle things around eventually.

It is a small, impractical choice. But personal sites are one of the few places left on the web where impractical choices are allowed to be the whole point.
